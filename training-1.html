Module 2 · What AI is, and isn't · 2 min

It predicts. It doesn't know.

Generative AI — Copilot, ChatGPT and others — predicts the most likely words, images, or code based on patterns in huge amounts of data. That makes it fast and fluent. It does not make it right.

Good at

Drafting, summarizing, rewording, brainstorming, explaining, and a first pass at analysis.

Bad at

Being right every time. It can invent part numbers, prices, dates, or facts — and sound completely sure. These are called hallucinations.

AI is already inside software you use. Outlook, Teams, the CRM, even browsers are adding AI features. Those count as AI tools too, and follow the same rules.

Module 4 · The ground rules · 4 min

Five rules. One gut check.

  1. Accountability stays human. AI-assisted work is your work.
  2. Protect confidential information. Data rules are covered in Training 2.
  3. Verify before you use. Check numbers, names, prices, and claims.
  4. Approved tools only. Covered next.
  5. Ask when unsure. Asking is always the right move.
The gut check: Would I put my name on this if I'd done it by hand?

Ask first — get approval before you:

  • Connect AI to company systems, email, files, or P21
  • Let AI take actions or make decisions on its own
  • Use AI for anything that talks directly to customers
  • Generate legal or financial documents
  • Build, train, or fine-tune AI, or build apps with AI (Retool is the only approved tool for this)
  • Create synthetic voice, images, or video of a real person — consent and disclosure required; impersonation is never allowed

Module 6 · Model-training settings · 2 min

Our data never trains their AI.

Many AI tools have a setting that lets the vendor use your conversations, files, or recordings to train or "improve" their models. At Richards Supply, that setting must always be off.

Why it matters

With it on, customer pricing, vendor terms, or internal conversations could end up in a vendor's training data. That can't be undone.

What it's called

Names vary: "Improve the model for everyone," "Help improve," "Data improvement," "Use my data for training." Learn the idea, not one label.

Who handles it

IT turns it off company-wide before a tool is approved. Copilot and ChatGPT Enterprise/Team don't train on our data under our agreements. Read.ai and Granola are approved only with data improvement off.

Your job

Don't change data-sharing or privacy settings. See an "improve the model" option turned on? Stop using it for company data and tell IT. If a tool won't let you turn it off, it isn't approved.

Module 7 · When something goes wrong · 2 min

Report it fast. Honesty is protected.

Pasted something you shouldn't have, or used an unapproved tool?

  1. Stop.
  2. Take a screenshot of what happened.
  3. Report it right away to IT or mattk@richardssupply.com.
  4. Don't try to fix or delete it yourself.
Self-reporting works. Report an accident promptly, with no data breach, and there's no discipline. Good-faith reporters are protected from retaliation.

What happens with repeated or serious violations

  • 1st: verbal warning and retraining
  • 2nd: written warning and loss of AI access
  • Repeated or serious: discipline up to termination

Privacy in company tools
AI use in company tools is monitored, and conversation history is kept. Don't expect privacy there, and don't delete your history.
